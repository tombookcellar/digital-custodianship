# A vision for Digital Custodianship
v0.1 - For comment

## Context and background

Our infrastructure is ageing. The gap between asset condition and the funding needed to keep our infrastructure in service is growing year on year. 

The standards and approaches we use to manage infrastructure were mostly defined at the advent of database systems in the latter decades of the 20th century, and we've got stuck with technologies and approaches that are 20 or more years old.

Recent advances in technology means that previous limitations in our ability to capture, store and process data no longer apply, but in a world of drones, smartphones, remote sensing, quantum technologies and Aritificial Intelligence the sector has no common north star to work towards. 

The Digital Custodianship Vision aims to align stakeholders so that we can articulate a shared end state for the role technology should play in managing infrastructure, and to accelerate innovation without constraining how it gets implemented and governed. 

We are inviting comment on this vision in the hope that others will apply these principals to their own initiatives to catalyse the adoption of technologies that reduce cost, increase resources or improve prioritisation to help our infrastructure last longer.

Whether your role is in infrastructurement management or innovation, please give us feedback on the definitions and principles set out below, and sign up to add your name in support of the Vision. 

## Addressing the condition crisis

In order to address the challenges of managing ageing infrastructure, we need a fundamental shift in how we think about data and planning, by moving away from monitoring condition to proactively managing risk.

Our [paper on Asset Custodianship](/pdfs/asset-custodianship.pdf) set out the different operating modes that asset owners work to and the four risks that need to be controlled. A key finding is that there is no optimal solution to managing ageing infrastructure when the available resources are less than what is required to maintain condition.

Many owners of ageing infrastructure are in a situation where asset condition has deteriorated beyond acceptable levels and don't have the resources to restore condition, and are therefore in a form of *crisis mode*, where the only option is to do the best they can with what they have and wave the red flag to try and secure more resources.

At the same time, we have seen a shift in how assets are managed; from career-long positions as municipal engineers responsible for a fixed number of assets which they knew intimately, to increased reliance on external engineering consultancies. Whilst consultancies bring a lot of value in terms of expertise, best-practice and innovation, we have lost that long-term personal stewardship for our assets.

We believe that digital technologies present an opportunity to reclaim that long-term perspective and sense of stewardship, whilst not losing access to the benefits that comes from organisations that directly share lessons learned across owners and portfolios.

We have coined the term *Digital Custodianship* to articulate the journey we expect the industry to go on from periodic qualitative condition scores supported by unstructured narratives and photos, to an integrated, long-term view of risk (consequence and likelihood) from deterioration and changes in stakeholder needs.

***Digital Custodianship*** is the proportionate implementation of technologies to extend the useful life of assets by

1. building quantified, longitudinal understanding of asset risk,
2. using algorithmic approaches to plan, prioritise & justify interventions, and
3. sharing and aggregating data to continuously improve sector-wide predictive models for asset risk.

We believe that by catalysing the adoption of emerging sensing and advanced computational tools by asset owners, and facilitating the next generation of models & technologies through better data sharing and improved commercial pathways, Digital Custodianship will be a key enabler for addressing the condition crisis.

## The Vision

Our vision for Digital Custodianship is:

> ***A world where asset owners can fully articulate their risk position and they are empowered to act on it.***

Not condition scores. Not compliance checklists. A clear, quantified account of likelihood and consequence, held with enough confidence to justify a decision, communicated in language that lands with the people who control the budget.

In that world:

- **Custodians know, not guess.**

  The aperture of possible futures for any given asset has narrowed, because longitudinal, quantified data describes how it's actually changing over time.

- **Interventions are purposeful.**

  Resources go where risk is real and rising, not where a proxy measure happens to look worst.

- **The case for investment is undeniable.**

  When the evidence says defer this repair five years and here is the consequence, defer it ten and here is the escalation, decision-makers can't wave it away as caution or professional pessimism.

- **The Condition Crisis is escapable.**

  The hardest part of crisis mode is that everything looks urgent and nothing can be prioritised with confidence. Quantified risk breaks that deadlock — and gives custodians the evidence to make the case for the additional resource that's the only real way out.

- **Success is observable.**

  Custodianship's central paradox is that success looks like nothing happening. Quantified data gives custodians a way to show the averted consequence, not just claim it.

## What is Digital Custodianship

![A diagram of Digital Custodianship](/images/digital-custodianship-diagram.png)

Digital Custodianship delivers six outcomes for an asset owner:

- **Continuity of knowledge** — the tacit, career-long familiarity a municipal engineer once held, held instead in data that outlasts any individual's tenure.
- **Timely monitoring** — knowing what's changing, not just what the last inspection found.
- **Proactive interventions** — acting on risk before it becomes visible, not reacting once it is.
- **Portfolio prioritisation** — knowing where, across hundreds or thousands of assets, attention and resource are actually needed most.
- **Stakeholder stewardship** — being able to explain risk in a way that resonates with the people who control funding and decisions.
- **Long-term sustainability** — infrastructure that keeps performing 5, 10, 15 years out, not just today.

None of that comes from a single technology. It comes from six capabilities, applied at the right scale for the asset in question:

- visual information captured in a structured way,
- sensing deployed where it earns its keep,
- quantified change tracked over time,
- data aggregation across sources and owners,
- computational diagnosis and probabilistic prediction,
- and machine learning that surfaces patterns and shares lessons across a portfolio.



#### The paradox this has to solve

Good custodianship, done well, produces an absence: no closures, no incidents, no headlines. That absence is very hard to fund. Nobody feels the disaster that didn't happen. This is the crying-wolf problem at the heart of infrastructure investment: the better the job, the harder the case for the next budget cycle.

Digital Custodianship doesn't remove that paradox, but it changes its shape. Quantified, longitudinal data is the evidence that the "nothing happened" was earned, not lucky. It's also the mechanism by which spend can genuinely fall over time. Data replaces guesswork, and guesswork is expensive: over-inspection, over-engineering, and precautionary restriction all cost money that better information can save. The role of data isn't just to unlock funding for action. It's to reduce how much funding is needed to find out what's actually going on in the first place.

## Principles for Digital Custodianship

Digital Custodianship isn't a system to buy or a specific set of technologies to deploy.

It is a set of principles that hold regardless of what you're actually implementing — whether that's an ISO 55000 asset management system, a new data standard, a monitoring programme, or a piece of technology built for asset owners. If you're doing any of that work, these are the principles worth bearing in mind.

1. **Risk over condition**

   Design for likelihood and consequence, not for condition proxies. A system built to produce a severity score is a different system to one built to answer "what happens if this fails, and how likely is that?" — and only the second one supports custodianship.

2. **Longitudinal over snapshot**

   A single inspection, however good, only ever describes a point in time. The aperture of possible futures only narrows when data builds a picture of change over time.

3. **Proportionate over universal**

   Not every asset needs the same level of technological intervention. The right question is always what level of technology a specific asset's risk profile justifies.

4. **Judgement over automation**

   Technology should extend what a custodian can see and predict, not substitute a black box for their judgement. Good systems make good judgement easier to exercise at scale — they don't remove the need for it.

5. **Aggregation over silos**

   Inspection, monitoring, incident, and usage data are more valuable together than apart, however well each performs in isolation. Similarly, no single estate holds enough data on its own for machine learning to find the patterns that matter. By bringing data together across owners new capabilities become possible.

6. **Stakeholders over specialists**

   Data that's technically correct but only intelligible to an engineer hasn't done its job. The purpose of quantifying risk is to make the case to the people who control resources, not just to satisfy the people who collected it.

## Enabling Digital Custodianship

This vision doesn't arrive through better technology alone. Much of what it needs already exists, in some form, from some vendor, but structural barriers and industry inertia is slowing the pace of adoption and collaboration.

In order to realise our vision we need to facilitate sector wide change in four areas:

1. **Ways to navigate what already exists and select the right technology for the problem**

    There's a huge amount out there, and it's genuinely difficult to evaluate the options and work out what, for this specific problem, would actually help make the case. Asset owners need help finding and trusting the right tool for the specific problem in front of them.

2. **Better adoption pathways within asset owners.**

    Identifying the right technology is one thing. Getting it through procurement, buying it, justifying it, and actually taking the decision to adopt is a genuinely painful process for owners to navigate — painful enough that, often, they simply don't.

3. **Easier routes to market for innovators and technology providers.**
  
    his is the flip side of adoption. Innovators and technology providers need pathways that let their technology become viable and sustainable — reaching the asset owners who need it, gaining traction, and getting the validation that lets them keep going, without which the tools that make this vision possible never reach the people who need them.

4. **System-level constructs fit for how much data now exists.**
  
    Much of today's asset management thinking, and the standards built on it, was designed in an era when data was scarce and expensive. Applying that thinking unchanged to a world where quantified, longitudinal data is genuinely achievable leaves most of its value on the table. Data standards, procurement frameworks, and asset management systems all need to catch up to what's now possible.

## Next steps

1. Build consensus around our vision for Digital Custodianship, bringing together regulators, owners, consultancies, supply chain and technology providers.
2. Create data standards for sharing asset data, with increasing maturity levels towards Digital Custodianship.
3. Convene technology providers to create a common voice, conceptual architectures and commercialisation channels to improve collaboration and access to markets and capital.
4. Build on the work of Project Moonshot to publish a catalogue of Digital Custodianship technologies spanning asset management, inspections, SHM, NDT and planning.
